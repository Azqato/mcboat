The Full Briefing

🦈🦈🦈 Maine

Shark Risk: Extremely High

Primary operating zone. Best coastline. Most sharks. Great white sightings increase every year. The exo-skeleton was designed with Maine in mind.

Notable: The Great White made Maine famous before we did.

🦈🦈🦈 Cape Cod, MA

Shark Risk: Maximum

Cape Cod has its own dedicated shark research organization. This is their territory. We are guests. The exo-skeleton is your invitation.

Notable: The Cape Cod White Shark Conservancy exists. That's a sentence.

🦈 New Hampshire

Shark Risk: Low-Medium

New Hampshire has 18 miles of coastline. Sharks are present but they find the small surface area underwhelming. They still show up.

State motto: "Live Free or Drown" (does not apply to the exo-skeleton experience).

🌲 Vermont

Shark Risk: Zero

Landlocked. We include Vermont because it was on the sticky note and we respect the original vision. No maritime operations planned.

Vermont: confirmed shark-free since forever. This is their only advantage.

🦈 Rhode Island

Shark Risk: Medium

The smallest state has proportionally larger relative shark encounters. The sharks here are also small but ambitious. Just like Rhode Island.

Block Island: technically accessible. Sharks aware.

🐟 Connecticut

Shark Risk: Low

Mostly regular fish. Some concerning ones. Connecticut is technically part of New England but the sharks keep forgetting it's there. We don't blame them.

Still in service area. Connecticut knows why.
